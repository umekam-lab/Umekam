# Property maintenance and drone services in Denmark: market scan of 4 problem/opportunity pairs (r3)

Access date for every source below: 2026-09-22. Publication dates are given where the URL, title or snippet shows one; otherwise "undated".
Labels: **[Fact]** = observed in a source; **[Estimate]** = researcher's rough figure, not from a source; **[Hypothesis]** = claim still to be tested. Evidence strength: Strong / Medium / Weak, as defined in the brief.

## Q1: Maintenance planning and building-condition management for andelsboligforeninger (A/B) and ejerforeninger (E/F)

### Takeaway
There is real, recurring spend here. Maintenance plans (vedligeholdelsesplaner) are sold at published prices of about 8,000–60,000 kr ex. moms by many engineering firms and administrators. They are legally required only for A/Bs founded on or after 1 July 2018, and elsewhere they are driven by bylaws (vedtægter), the rules on key-information forms (nøgleoplysninger), valuation practice and what lenders and estate agents expect. The market is crowded: administrators such as DEAS and Newsec bundle the service, and digital plan platforms already exist (Plan 1, Min Boligforening, Ejendom.com, Unyti). A newcomer's best angle is probably not writing the plan itself, which needs building expertise. It is more likely the volunteer board's job of keeping the plan, the documentation and the contractor follow-up alive between the 3–5-year updates. That angle is a hypothesis.

### Cited Findings
**Legal requirements (A/B)**
- [Fact] The statutory vedligeholdelsesplan requirement, including its revision and updating, applies only to A/Bs founded on or after 1 July 2018. The plan must list the larger maintenance works needed for sound operation, when they will be done, and a cost estimate. The prices do not have to come from obtained bids. — [Vejledning nr. 9507 af 22/06/2018 om vedligeholdelsesplaner (retsinformation, "gældende" per 2026 print)](https://www.retsinformation.dk/api/pdf/202268) (dated 22/06/2018); [retsinformation ELI version](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/retsinfo/2018/9507); [Andelsportal guide](https://www.andelsportal.dk/guides/guide-vedligeholdelsesplan/) (undated)
- [Fact] For these new A/Bs, a plan covering at least 15 years must exist before the association agrees to buy the property, and it must be updated at least every 5 years. — [EjendomDanmark: Regler for vedligeholdelsesplan i nye A/B](https://ejd.dk/nyheder/praeciserende-regler-for-vedligeholdelsesplaner-i-nye-andelsboligforeninger-2) (undated); [DEAS: Vedligeholdelsesplan](https://deas.dk/da/bestyrelse-i-andelsboligforening/vedligeholdelsesplan) (undated), which says "must be updated at least every 5 years"
- [Fact] The rule comes from a working-group recommendation that an A/B should prepare a plan at formation, update it at least every 5 years, and cover at least 15 years ahead. — [Folketinget ERU Alm.del Bilag 74, lovforslag andelsboligloven (session 2017-18)](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20171/almdel/ERU/bilag/74/1829959.pdf)
- [Fact] Key-information forms (nøgleoplysningsskemaer) under the rules in force since 1 July 2018 come in three parts: (1) a form for the association, completed once a year and reusable across sales; (2) a form for each sale; (3) a declaration of material changes. The maintenance plan is among the documents to be handed over. — [Andelsportal: Ændringer i kravene til nøgleoplysningsskemaer](https://www.andelsportal.dk/nyheder/aendringer-i-kravene-til-noegleoplysningsskemaer/) (undated); [Andelsportal: Nye regler fra 1. juli 2018](https://www.andelsportal.dk/nyheder/nye-regler-noegleoplysninger/) (undated); [ABF: Nøgleoplysninger](https://www.abf-rep.dk/radgivning/lov-og-fakta/kob-og-salg-af-andelsbolig/nogleoplysninger/) (undated)
- [Fact] Each sale requires digital reporting of specified key fields (A3, Z2, Z3, Z9, Z18, Z19) to andelsboliginfo.dk no later than 30 days after the end of the quarter in which the sale took place. — [Socialministeriet: Vejledning om udlevering af nøgleoplysninger og indberetning](https://www.sm.dk/arbejdsomraader/byggeri-og-boliglovgivning/andelsboliger/koeb-og-salg/vejledning-om-udlevering-af-noegleoplysninger) (undated); [BEK nr 1392 af 14/06/2021, now marked "Historisk"](https://www.retsinformation.dk/api/pdf/224329); [newer bekendtgørelse om oplysningspligt (lovtidende)](https://www.lovtidende.dk/api/pdf/248837) (date not seen)
- [Fact] Estate agents use an extra "Andelsboligskema" alongside the key-information forms, which suggests buyers and agents ask for more than the statutory minimum. — [Dansk Ejendomsmæglerforening: Andelsboligskema v2.4 (PDF)](https://www.de.dk/media/mqfmosst/v-2-4_-_andelsboligskema_nyt.pdf) (undated)
- [Fact] The "Blackstone law" took effect on 1 July 2020. It lets an A/B keep (freeze) its most recent valuer's valuation (valuarvurdering) or public valuation dated before 1 July 2020, for an unlimited period. An association that switches to a valuation made after 1 July 2020 loses the freeze. — [Cobblestone: "Blackstone-loven" er en realitet (2020)](https://cobblestone.dk/nyheder/2020/blackstone-loven-er-en-realitet); [Cobblestone: Fastfrysning af valuarvurdering (2021)](https://cobblestone.dk/nyheder/2021/fastfrysning-af-valuarvurdering); [ABF: Fastfrysning af valuarvurdering](https://www.abf-rep.dk/radgivning/lov-og-fakta/okonomi/fastfrysning-af-valuarvurdering/) (undated); [Newsec: et år med den nye boliglov](https://www.newsec.dk/property-asset-management/foreninger/nyhedsbreve/fastfrysning-af-vurderinger-i-andelsboligforeninger-et-aar-med-den-nye-boliglov/) (undated)
- [Fact, source not pinned down] A search summary said that after a law change effective 15 April 2024, a valuarvurdering is valid for 42 months, so in practice a new valuation is needed about every 3 years, and the property's condition is assessed against its maintenance plan or condition report. The summary drew on several pages (ABF andelsværdiberegning, Andelsportal valuarvurdering guide, Min Boligforening 2023, Vest Administrationen), and I could not tie the claim to one of them. **Verify before use.** — [ABF: Andelsværdiberegning](https://www.abf-rep.dk/radgivning/lov-og-fakta/okonomi/andelsvaerdiberegning/) (undated); [Andelsportal: Valuarvurdering](https://www.andelsportal.dk/guides/valuarvurdering-hvad-skal-man-vaere-opmaerksom-paa/) (undated)
- [Fact] A valuer must hold a Diplom i Vurdering, have at least 2 years' experience valuing residential rental property, and carry liability insurance. — [BEK nr 978 af 2018 om vurdering ved valuar](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2018/978)

**Legal requirements (E/F)**
- [Fact] The ejerlejlighedsloven (LBK nr. 1713 af 16/08/2021, per the snippet) requires ejerforeninger to carry out the necessary maintenance and renovation. ### Inferences
- **Problem [Hypothesis]:** Volunteer boards own a maintenance plan (often a PDF produced every 3–5 years), a budget, the key-information forms, the valuation link and contractor jobs. In between updates the plan goes stale, knowledge leaves with outgoing board members, and the next board struggles to evidence the property's condition to buyers, banks and valuers.
- **User:** the board (chair, treasurer, "house/building" member). **Buyer and budget holder:** the board, within a budget approved by the general meeting. The administrator often controls the channel and may bundle or recommend suppliers. **Trigger:** a new A/B, an update due every 3–5 years, a valuation cycle, an administrator change, major damage, board turnover, or a pending sale. **Frequency:** the plan every 3–5 years; the software or workflow need is continuous (this last point is a hypothesis).
- **Alternatives:** do nothing (old PDF plus spreadsheet), the administrator's bundled plan, an engineer's plan, or Plan 1, Min Boligforening, Ejendom.com or Unyti.
- **Cost of doing nothing:** only vendor claims were found: "up to 30%" higher maintenance cost (Plan 1) and emergency repairs. No independent quantification was found.
- **Evidence strength: Medium–Strong.** More than 10 providers publish or offer prices, a statutory requirement covers a subset of A/Bs, and administrators monetise the service. No transaction volumes were found.
- **Size [Estimate]:** about 9,000 A/Bs plus E/Fs. There are more than 300,000 ejerlejligheder, so plausibly well over 10,000 E/Fs, but many are small, have no CVR number or run without an administrator, which is why eStatistik's 2,463 is an undercount (an inference). Assuming 9,000 A/Bs each buy a plan every ~4 years at a ~20,000 kr midpoint gives roughly 45 million kr a year for A/B plans alone. This is illustrative only, not a sourced market size.
- **Why switch; newcomer advantage [Hypothesis]:** a "living plan" for the volunteer board. It would include AI-assisted intake of existing PDF plans, inspection photos and invoices; task and contractor follow-up; automatic pre-filling of the key-information forms and board handover packs; and optional drone photo documentation (linking to Q2). **Why incumbents might not copy it:** administrators earn fees from the plan work and from building-project administration (byggesagsadministration), so they have little reason to make boards self-sufficient. Plan 1 and Ejendom.com, however, could easily add AI features, so this barrier is weak.
- **Founder fit: good.** Board governance experience, a documentation/workflow product background (Lovable + Supabase), property-related work and direct-sales strength all fit. The gap is building-technical credibility: an engineering or building-technician (bygningskonstruktør) partner would be needed for condition assessments.
- **Capital [Estimate]:** test with customer interviews plus a clickable prototype for under 25k DKK; launch an MVP with 3–5 pilot boards in the 25–150k DKK tier.
- **Red flags:** a crowded field with an existing digital category leader (Plan 1); administrators as gatekeepers; small, slow, AGM-driven budgets and volunteer decision cycles; the law only requires plans for post-2018 A/Bs; a pure software play may meet low willingness to pay.
- **Preliminary ratings (1–5):** demand evidence 4 · ability to reach buyers 3 · founder fit 4 · contribution margin 3 · differentiation 2 · execution feasibility 3 · capital exposure 4 · growth potential 3 · **confidence: medium**

## Q2: Drone roof, facade and gutter inspection as a service in Denmark

### Takeaway
Customers do pay: at least 6 Danish providers publish prices of about 3,000–9,500 kr per inspection, far below scaffolding at 15,000–25,000 kr for a house. But the market is crowded with dronefotografer, engineers and even gutter cleaners, and regulation is tightening. The DK-STS-03 declaration expired on 1 January 2026, electronic identification (Remote-ID) is proposed for all drones over 250 g from 1 January 2027, and Denmark imposed a nationwide ban on civil drones for five days in September–October 2025. A stand-alone drone-inspection business looks commoditised. It is more defensible as a data-capture feature inside Q1.

**Regulation**

- [Fact] Liability insurance is a legal requirement for all drones regardless of weight, according to secondary sources. The July 2025 exemption applies only to private mini-drones. — [droner.dk regler](https://droner.dk/regler-for-droneflyvning) (undated); [denbedstedrone.dk](https://denbedstedrone.dk/regler) (undated)
- [Fact] With the **A2 CofC** and a C2-class drone, a pilot may fly within 30 m of uninvolved people, or 5 m in low-speed mode, but not over them. — [droneregler.dk: A2](https://www.droneregler.dk/dronecertifikat/a2-kompetencecertifikat); [overblik over certifikater](https://www.droneregler.dk/dronecertifikat/overblik-over-dronecertifikaterne) (undated)
- [Fact] In the **specific category / STS** (C5/C6 drones), the operator writes an operations manual, meets the drone, training and operational requirements, and files a declaration with Trafikstyrelsen. — [droneregler.dk: erhverv](https://www.droneregler.dk/erhvervsdroneflyvning) (undated)

### Inferences
- **Problem:** roofs, gutters and facades are expensive and dangerous to inspect by scaffold or lift, and the resulting documentation is poor.
- **Users and buyers:** A/B and E/F boards (budget via the maintenance budget), administrators, bygningssagkyndige (subcontracting), roofers (pre-quote surveys, as a sales tool) and insurers (claims). **Triggers:** maintenance-plan updates, storm damage, insurance claims, pre-renovation surveys and pre-sale checks. **Frequency:** mostly one-off or every few years per building. Insurers and administrators with large portfolios buy in campaigns (a hypothesis, supported only by B3D's campaign pricing).
- **Evidence strength: Medium.** Many providers publish prices; no Danish tenders were found. Named insurer programmes were **not found**.
- **Saturation:** high. Photographers, engineers, gutter cleaners and course sellers all compete at 3,000–10,000 kr a job, and the entry barrier (open category plus A2) is low. Differentiation would have to come from analysis and reporting integrated into maintenance plans, not from flying.

After DK-STS-03 expired, legacy drones without C-marking are restricted in urban areas, and Remote-ID from 2027 may require retrofits or new equipment. Launch with a compliant C2 or thermal drone, insurance, training and reporting software in the 25–150k DKK tier. Equipment prices were **not researched** and must be verified.
- **Red flags:** commoditised pricing; more regulation (Remote-ID 2027, logbooks); the risk of security-driven blanket bans like September 2025; weather; GDPR exposure when filming residential courtyards; liability for missed defects when a "report" is sold rather than just photos.
- **Best use [Hypothesis]:** as the data-capture layer feeding a Q1 "living maintenance plan", sold to boards and administrators, not as a stand-alone service.

## Q3: "Orphaned" home solar PV systems (installer bankrupt or no longer servicing)

### Takeaway
**Evidence is thin; low confidence.** Only two Danish signals were found: a trade-press report of a Danish solar-company bankruptcy (BlueSolar) and an electrician marketing help to customers whose supplier went bankrupt. The strongest evidence of the pattern is Norwegian: Solcellespesialisten (December 2025) and Solintegra (March 2026) went bankrupt, and a Norwegian competitor launched a service package for customers affected by bankruptcies. The problem is plausible in Denmark but unquantified. The main gaps are the installed base, number of bankruptcies, complaints and prices.
