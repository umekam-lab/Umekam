## Pair 1 — Hull fouling and antifouling pollution: brush boat-wash stations or diver/robot hull cleaning instead of biocidal paint

### Takeaway
Sweden shows that brush boat-washers work and are paid for: about 100–200 wash platforms and brush washers, and about 400–600 SEK (≈270–400 DKK) per wash, 2–3 times a season on the east coast. That demand rests on a regulatory push: since 2002 no copper paints have been approved for leisure boats on the Swedish east coast. **Denmark has no equivalent copper ban for saltwater boats.** Biocidal paint is banned only on boats under 200 kg (with exceptions) and in fresh water. The search found **no Danish brush-wash installation**. The leading Swedish equipment maker (Rentunder) is shrinking and loss-making. Demand evidence for Denmark is therefore weak, and the concept needs a marina partner, capital of about 0.3–1.7 million DKK and wastewater approval. Diver hull cleaning is legally a commercial dive (a PADI certificate is not enough), and the Danish Environmental Protection Agency (Miljøstyrelsen) advises against scrubbing painted hulls in the water.

### Cited Findings

**Danish regulation (antifouling, washing, wastewater)**
- [Fact] Biocidal antifouling ("bundmaling") is banned on leisure boats and dinghies under 200 kg; these must be taken ashore or use biocide-free alternatives. Biocidal paint is banned on boats used mainly in fresh water. Irgarol, diuron and TBT are never allowed. Biocidal paint must be approved by Miljøstyrelsen. The under-200 kg ban does not apply to wooden boats in salt water, or to boats with a fixed berth in harbours rated A or B in the insurance industry's harbour register. — [Miljøstyrelsen: Regler om bundmaling](https://mst.dk/borger/hjem-og-have/bil-og-anden-transport/bundmaling-til-fritidsbaade/regler-om-bundmaling) (undated; accessed 2026-09-22)
- [Fact] The legal basis appears as BEK nr 1598 af 09/11/2020 in search listings for this topic. The search extract says the rules were "most recently updated in 2020". A separate MST Q&A extract says the rules were "last updated in late 2017". **These conflict, and I could not open the text to check.** Historical basis: BEK 1278/2005 on restricting import, sale and use of biocidal antifouling. — [Retsinformation BEK 1598/2020](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2020/1598) (09/11/2020); [BEK 1278/2005](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2005/1278) (2005); [MST Spørgsmål og svar om bundmaling](https://mst.dk/borger/hjem-og-have/bil-og-anden-transport/bundmaling-til-fritidsbaade/spoergsmaal-og-svar-om-bundmaling) (undated)
- [Fact] Copper compounds (copper thiocyanate, copper(I) oxide) are the main biocides. In Denmark zinc pyrithione is also used, often combined with copper. Copper does not break down, builds up in sediments, and has been found in Danish coastal waters in amounts harmful to animals and plants. — [MST: Miljø og bundmaling til fritidsbåde](https://mst.dk/borger/hjem-og-have/bil-og-anden-transport/bundmaling-til-fritidsbaade/miljoe-og-bundmaling-til-fritidsbaade) (undated)
- [Fact] Copper and some other biocides remain allowed in paints sold in Denmark, but requirements on boat owners for applying and removing paint have been tightened. Washing or scrubbing a painted hull increases copper release. Owners should avoid washing or scrubbing a painted hull while the boat is in the water, use a tarp when sanding or painting, and use harbour equipment or harbour-approved methods to remove paint. A soft brush with fresh water, or gentle high-pressure washing, is usually enough. — [MST Spørgsmål og svar om bundmaling](https://mst.dk/borger/hjem-og-have/bil-og-anden-transport/bundmaling-til-fritidsbaade/spoergsmaal-og-svar-om-bundmaling) (undated); [Minbåd: Miljøstyrelsens tips](https://minbaad.dk/nyhed/archive/2021/22/march/article/miljstyrelsens-tips-til-at-begrnse-udledning-af-bundmalingens-biocider/) (2021)
- [Fact] At least one Danish harbour's wash area for leisure boats has wastewater treatment (sand trap, oil separator, flocculation and filtration), and it is covered by an environmental approval before discharge is allowed. MST publishes a guidance note for the wash-area order (vaskepladsbekendtgørelsen). Harbour bylaws also regulate how paint is applied and removed (example: Ålbæk Havn). — [Holstebro-Struer Havn document, dma.mst.dk](https://dma.mst.dk/vis-sag/download/9614f2bb-7330-4d37-ada6-bd258be81771) (undated); [MST vejledning til vaskepladsbekendtgørelsen (PDF)](https://mst.dk/media/5jbnyqrb/vejledning-til-vaskepladsbekendtgoerelsen.pdf) (undated); [Ålbæk Havn: Påføring og fjernelse af bundmaling](https://aalbaekhavn.frederikshavn.dk/velkommen-til-aalbaek-havn/havneregulativ/paafoering-og-fjernelse-af-bundmaling) (undated)
- [Fact] Changes in 2024–2025: a trade article says the rules/product line-up were "renewed in 2025". In 2024–2025 several Hempel, Jotun and International paints were withdrawn because of changed environmental rules and approval procedures. (It is not clear which of the two listed articles the extract came from.) — [Motorbåds nyt: Det må du bruge af bundmaling i 2025](https://motorbaadsnyt.dk/nyhed/archive/2025/09/february/article/det-maa-du-bruge-af-bundmaling-i-2025/) (Feb 2025); [ODMS bådudstyr](https://odms-baadudstyr.dk/nyheder/7-faa-overblik-over-bundmaling-til-din-baad-typer-og-miljoevenlige-alternativer/) (undated)
- [Fact] MST published a 2003 study on washing and sanding biocidal antifouling during autumn haul-out, and a 2002 review of existing methods. — [MST 2003 report](https://www2.mst.dk/udgiv/publikationer/2003/87-7972-495-7/html/kap04.htm) (2003); [MST 2002 kap. 4](https://www2.mst.dk/udgiv/publikationer/2002/87-7972-126-5/html/kap04.htm) (2002)

**Swedish precedent**
- [Fact] In 2002 the Swedish Chemicals Agency (Kemikalieinspektionen, KemI) stopped approving copper antifouling for the east coast. No copper paints are approved for leisure boats there. The west coast allows higher copper leakage. Biocide-free paints need no approval. — [KemI: Regler för båtbottenfärger](https://www.kemi.se/lagar-och-regler/lagstiftningar-inom-kemikalieomradet/bekampningsmedel/biocider/vanliga-typer-av-biocidprodukter/batbottenfarger) (undated); [Båtunionen/KemI: 40 års reglering (PDF)](https://batunionen.se/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/40-ars-reglering-av-biocidfarger-i-Sverige.pdf) (Oct 2025)
- [Fact] Sweden has "between 100 and 200" wash platforms and brush washers (Havs- och vattenmyndigheten, HaV). Wash platforms have grown fast in western Sweden. HaV has published guidelines for hull washing of leisure boats (revised Dec 2014). — [HaV press release via Mynewsdesk](http://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/havochvatten/pressreleases/hav-s-riktlinjer-foer-baatbottentvaett-maalet-aer-att-faa-bort-de-giftiga-faergerna-1154864) (undated, probably 2014–2015); [HaV: Kraftig ökning av spolplattor](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/havochvatten/pressreleases/kraftig-oekning-av-spolplattor-foer-baatar-i-vaest-728439) (undated); [Länsstyrelsen/HaV riktlinjer PDF](https://www.lansstyrelsen.se/download/18.2e0f9f621636c844027d258/1527507748880/Rapport-2012-10-riktlinjer-b%C3%A5tbottentv%C3%A4ttning-av-fritidsb%C3%A5tar.pdf) (rev. Dec 2014). **The count is probably at least 10 years old; no current figure found.**
- [Fact] Prices and frequency: on the east coast 2–3 washes per season usually suffice, at about 400–600 SEK per wash (≈270–400 DKK). Prices range from about 100 SEK per metre up to about 500 SEK per wash. One club offers 350 SEK (≈235 DKK) to members because the wash is co-funded by state support. Some sites sell season packages of 3–4 washes at a discount. Advice is to wash at least twice a season, the first time in the second half of July or early August. — Search extracts from [Tyresö kommun: Båtbottentvätt](https://www.tyreso.se/boende--miljo/trafik/fritidsbatar-och-batklubbar/batbottentvatt.html) (undated); [Näsbyvikens Båtsällskap](https://www.nasbyviken.se/batbottentvatt/) (undated); [Gustavsbergs gästhamn](https://nautilus.se/eng/hyra-bat/bottentvatt-bat/) (undated); [Båtmiljö: Borsttvätt](https://batmiljo.se/mala-baten/sluta-mala-baten/borsttvatt/) (undated). The extract does not say which page each figure came from.
- [Estimate] Season cost per boat in Sweden: 2–3 × 400–600 SEK = 800–1,800 SEK ≈ **540–1,210 DKK per season**.
- [Fact] Rentunder (Drive-in Boatwash; listed in Sweden) sells two washer models: a large one for motor- and sailboats at **2.5 million SEK (≈1.68 million DKK)** and a smaller one at **0.5 million SEK (≈335,000 DKK)**. ### Inferences
- **Problem, user, buyer.** [Hypothesis] Users are keelboat owners in saltwater marinas. The problem is copper/zinc pollution plus the yearly labour and cost of painting. The **buyer and budget holder for a washer** is the marina (municipal, self-owning or club-run) or a private operator who needs a site agreement. **Boat owners pay per wash.** The trigger is a marina environmental policy or funding, owner demand, or future regulation. The washer is a one-off capital purchase; washes happen 2–3 times a season (Swedish benchmark).
- **Why the business case is weak in Denmark (the key point).** [Hypothesis] Danish boats are normally lifted out for winter anyway. A brush-wash season (≈540–1,210 DKK at Swedish price levels) therefore replaces only the paint and painting labour, not the crane cost (≈600–1,850 DKK per lift). With copper paint still legal in Danish salt water, most owners would switch only for environmental reasons or convenience. Sweden's volumes grew out of a ban.
- **Doing nothing / current alternatives.** Keep painting with approved biocidal paint (legal). Use biocide-free paint plus manual scrubbing on land. Have SejlRent clean in the water with a drone: [Estimate] a 30 ft boat costs 30 × 80 + 695 = 3,095 DKK per visit. That is roughly 8–11 times the price of a Swedish brush wash, but it needs no fixed site.
- **Cost of leaving the problem unresolved.** Environmental (copper builds up in sediment, per MST) and fouling (lost speed and fuel). The fuel/speed cost has **not been quantified in any source found**.
- **Evidence strength.** Sweden: **Medium–Strong** (published prices, a listed equipment maker, 100–200 installations). Denmark: **Weak** (no installations, no tenders, no Danish prices for brush washing). The Danish price evidence that does exist is for drone cleaning (1 competitor, public price).
- **Why switch; what a newcomer can offer; why incumbents may not copy.** [Hypothesis] Marinas could market "copper-free harbour" status and reduce sediment contamination and dredging costs (the dredging-cost link is not verified). A newcomer could offer an operator model: supply, run and bill the washer, with no capital outlay for the marina. Paint makers and chandlers earn from paint, so they have no incentive to copy.
- **Diver/robot hull cleaning variant.** [Inference from sourced rules] Paid in-water cleaning by a diver counts as a work dive under the Diving Act (dykkerloven) and needs a commercial diver certificate from the Danish Maritime Authority (Søfartsstyrelsen); a PADI certificate does not count (see Pair 2). Separately, MST advises against scrubbing painted hulls in the water. So in-water cleaning is legally comfortable only for biocide-free hulls, and the addressable base is tiny today.
- **Founder fit.** [Hypothesis] Low to moderate. Diving skills do not help run a fixed washer. Direct sales to harbour boards fits. Limited weekly time conflicts with a seasonal operation that needs staffing (April–October).
- **Capital [Estimate].**
  - To test: 10,000–40,000 DKK for a survey of owners and marinas, a Swedish site visit, and a letter of intent from one marina.
  - To launch: small Rentunder unit ≈335,000 DKK, large ≈1.68 million DKK, plus installation, pontoon or site work, and approval of wastewater or sludge handling. Those costs were not found; a guess is +100,000–500,000 DKK.
  - [Estimate] Payback at Swedish price levels: 335,000 DKK ÷ about 300 DKK per wash ≈ 1,100 washes before operating costs. At 2–3 washes per boat that is about 370–560 boat-seasons.
- **Red flags.** Weak regulatory push in Denmark. The key supplier is contracting and wants to exit its listing. Revenue is seasonal (about 5 months). Site depends on a marina's consent. Unknown wastewater and permit burden. Converting from biocidal paint requires stripping the old paint (cost not found). [Hypothesis] Danish waters are saltier than the Swedish east coast, so barnacle pressure is higher and more washes may be needed.
- **Preliminary ratings (1–5).** Demand evidence 2 · Ability to reach buyers 3 · Founder fit 2 · Contribution margin 3 · Differentiation 3 · Execution feasibility 2 · Capital exposure 1 · Growth potential 3 · **Confidence: low–medium**.

## Pair 2 — Inspecting moorings, jetties and harbour infrastructure for marinas, sailing clubs and small harbours with divers or small underwater drones (ROVs)

### Takeaway
Paid underwater inspection exists in Denmark. Several commercial diving firms do jetty and quay inspections, and one publishes a price of 3,000 DKK per hour, assistant and travel extra. **But inspection by diving legally requires a Søfartsstyrelsen commercial diver certificate; the founder's PADI qualification does not count.** No Danish legal requirement for regular mooring inspection was found; only insurer guidance (check at least once a year). A small ROV (≈19,000–180,000 DKK) avoids the diving rules (no ROV certification was found) and could undercut a dive team for visual checks. Demand is real but small, seasonal and mostly unregulated.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] Under the Diving Act (dykkerloven), all dives that count as work dives must be done by a commercial diver. Commercial diving work may only be done by holders of a commercial diver certificate (erhvervsdykkercertifikat). The Act's purpose is to protect "persons using diving equipment". — [Danskelove: Dykkerloven](https://danskelove.dk/dykkerloven) (undated); [Altomteknik: Erhvervsdykning](https://altomteknik.dk/indhold/marine-og-offshore/erhvervsdykning/) (undated)
- [Fact] Søfartsstyrelsen issues the certificate. A 30 m SCUBA certificate is issued after an exam held by Søfartsstyrelsen. Søfartsstyrelsen approves diving schools. Courses exist for 30 m, 9 m, diving assistant, first aid and nitrox. Divers need a medical from an approved diving doctor. — [Søfartsstyrelsen: Erhvervsdykning](https://www.soefartsstyrelsen.dk/sikkerhed-til-soes/erhvervsdykning) (undated); [Søfartsstyrelsen: Dykkeruddannelse](https://www.soefartsstyrelsen.dk/sikkerhed-til-soes/erhvervsdykning/dykkeruddannelse) (undated); [Søfartsstyrelsen: Lægeundersøgelse](https://www.soefartsstyrelsen.dk/sikkerhed-til-soes/erhvervsdykning/laegeundersoegelse-af-dykkere) (undated)
- [Fact] Safe execution is governed by BEK 1395/2013 on safe diving work. The dive leader does no work tasks, supervises, and cannot delegate responsibility. — [Retsinformation BEK 1395/2013](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2013/1395) (2013); [Søfartsstyrelsen vejledning til BEK 1395](https://www.soefartsstyrelsen.dk/publikationer-og-vejledninger/vejledninger/2014/mar/vejledning-til-bekendtgoerelse-nr-1395-om-dykkerarbejdets-sikre-udfoerelse) (Mar 2014). The Danish minimum team size was **not found** in the extracts. For comparison, Norway requires at least 1 dive leader, 1 diver, 1 reserve diver and 2 dive assistants — [Lovdata: Forskrift om dykking](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SFO/forskrift/1990-11-30-944) (1990, amended).

### Inferences
- **Problem, user, buyer.** [Hypothesis] Marinas and clubs need to know the state of moorings (chains, blocks, shackles), jetty piles (shipworm, corrosion) and quay walls, to avoid boats breaking loose and structural failures. Users are harbour masters and club technical committees. Buyers and budget holders are the harbour board or municipality (municipal harbours) and the club board. Private buoy owners pay for their own moorings. Triggers: spring launch, after storms, insurance claims, renovation planning, and changes in harbour-master staff. Frequency is annual at most.
- **Current alternatives / doing nothing.** Harbour staff pull chains up on land; members snorkel or dive informally (legally risky if paid); or a certified dive firm is hired (≈3,000 DKK/h plus assistant and travel). The cost of leaving problems unresolved (breakaways, jetty collapse) was **not quantified in any source found**.
- **Evidence strength: Medium.** Several named incumbents and one public hourly price. No tenders or volumes found for marinas specifically.
- **ROV route.** [Hypothesis] ROV operation appears to fall outside the Diving Act, which covers persons using diving equipment. **No Danish ROV certification requirement was found**, but this should be confirmed with Søfartsstyrelsen. A one-person ROV inspection with video and a written condition report could be priced below a certified dive team. [Hypothesis] Danish harbour water is often murky, which may limit visual ROV inspection; sonar options add cost.
- **Why switch; newcomer improvement; incumbent response.** [Hypothesis] Faster booking, a standard fixed-price "mooring health report" with photos and GPS, cheaper than a dive team for simple visual checks. Incumbent dive firms target larger construction jobs, but they can easily buy ROVs too, so the moat is low.
- **Founder fit.** [Hypothesis] Moderate. A PADI instructor understands the underwater environment and sells to boating people, but **cannot legally dive for pay without the commercial certificate (about 49,000 DKK excl. VAT plus the exam and medical)**. The ROV route fits limited weekly time.
- **Capital [Estimate].** To test: about 20,000–30,000 DKK (FIFISH V6-class ROV at ≈19,000 DKK plus accessories), with 3–5 pilot inspections at friendly clubs. To launch: 60,000–250,000 DKK (Blueye X3-class ROV ≈181,500 DKK, liability insurance, trailer or boat). Insurance cost was not found.
- **Red flags.** No mandatory inspection regime, so the purchase is discretionary. Small tickets. Seasonal (spring). Marinas are volunteer-run and cost-sensitive. Water visibility. Dive firms are the natural sellers of the same service.
- **Preliminary ratings (1–5).** Demand evidence 2 · Ability to reach buyers 3 · Founder fit 3 · Contribution margin 4 · Differentiation 2 · Execution feasibility 3 · Capital exposure 4 · Growth potential 2 · **Confidence: low**.

## Pair 3 — Holiday-home (sommerhus) remote monitoring plus physical inspection ("tilsyn") subscription for distant owners

### Takeaway
The market is large and already paying. About 224,000 Danish families own a sommerhus (DST 2025). Many local inspection firms publish prices, but these are **very low: about 695–1,150 DKK per year for weekly visits**. Falck sells a national inspection product. Alarm companies charge several hundred per month (Verisure Norway: from 549 NOK/month; Danish prices not found). Risk is concentrated in winter: more than 40% of sommerhus break-ins happen November–February, and burst pipes, mould and break-ins are the most common damage. The opportunity is real (**Medium** evidence), but price points are low and competition is local and fragmented, so margins depend on route density plus a sensor subscription.

### Inferences
- **Problem, user, buyer.** [Hypothesis] Owners who are away (often in winter) cannot detect frost bursts, leaks, break-ins, damp or mould, storm damage or power cuts in time. User, buyer and budget holder are the same person: the owner household, paying out of pocket. Triggers: buying a house, a previous damage event, autumn/winter start (September–October), or an insurer's advice. Bought as an annual or seasonal subscription.
- **Price level.** [Estimate] At 695–1,150 DKK per season or year for weekly visits, each visit earns only about **20–40 DKK** (e.g., 695 DKK ÷ about 23 weeks ≈ 30 DKK). [Hypothesis] That implies drive-by exterior checks bundled into a dense local route. **A newcomer cannot compete on price for visits alone.** Money must come from sensors or monitoring (alarm-type monthly fees of a few hundred DKK) and paid call-outs (≈399–550 DKK per visit).
- **Doing nothing / current alternatives.** Winterise and hope; ask neighbours; install self-managed sensors (prices for Shelly, Aqara and Netatmo kits **not found**); buy an alarm (Verisure or Sector Alarm); hire a local inspector; rent through an agency (commission 25–32%). The cost of damage is not quantified in any source found; F&P and Alm. Brand only say damage clusters in winter and is found late.
- **Evidence strength: Medium.** Many competitors with public prices, plus a national player (Falck). There is no direct sales-volume data.
- **Why switch; newcomer improvement; incumbent response.** [Hypothesis] Local inspectors check weekly but not in real time. Alarm firms detect but do not fix, and are aimed at year-round homes. A newcomer could combine cheap leak, temperature, humidity and power sensors with local response (turn off water, dry out, call a tradesperson), and give owners a photo report in an app. The founder's software product-owner experience fits here. Small local firms lack the software; alarm companies lack local handymen. Falck is the main threat because it already bundles inspection.
- **Founder fit.** [Hypothesis] Moderate to good for direct sales and product design. Weak on the physical side if the founder does not live in a sommerhus cluster: each visit requires physical presence, and demand peaks in winter. Partnering with existing local inspectors (white-label monitoring) could fit limited time.
- **Capital [Estimate].** To test: about 10,000–30,000 DKK (sensor kits for 5–10 pilot houses plus a landing page; kit prices not verified). To launch: about 50,000–150,000 DKK (vehicle, insurance, software or platform, stock of sensors).
- **Red flags.** Very low willingness to pay for visits; fragmented local competition; Falck's brand; liability if damage is missed; route density needed; winter workload peak; storms can create simultaneous call-outs.
- **Preliminary ratings (1–5).** Demand evidence 4 · Ability to reach buyers 3 · Founder fit 3 · Contribution margin 2 · Differentiation 3 · Execution feasibility 4 · Capital exposure 4 · Growth potential 3 · **Confidence: medium**.
