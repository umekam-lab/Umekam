## Pair 1 — Tool theft from tradespeople's vans (håndværkere)

### Takeaway
The problem is real and people already pay to solve it: about 3,618 police-reported thefts from vans in 2025, more than DKK 760m in insurance payouts over 2020–2024, and one in four installer firms hit within a year. But spending already goes to a crowded, mature group of Danish lock installers that are tied into insurers, dealers and wholesalers, and the theft trend fell about 20% in 2025. A newcomer would have little to differentiate on.

### Cited Findings

**Size of the problem, trend and who is hit**

### Inferences
- **Problem / user / buyer.** [Hyp] User: electricians, plumbers and carpenters, plus their van drivers. Buyer and budget holder: the owner in small firms, or the fleet/service manager in larger installers. Triggers: a break-in (often repeated, as with Nim VVS), a new van, an insurer's discount or requirement, a dealer upsell at purchase. Frequency: once per van (a van lock is a one-off purchase), with a replacement cycle tied to the van (about 4–6 years, **not verified**).
- **Alternatives.** [Fact/Hyp] Doing nothing plus insurance (claims about DKK 40k, but excess, downtime and lost jobs are not covered); emptying the van overnight; DNA marking; GPS trackers; factory deadlocks; dealer-fitted locks.
- **Payback.** [Est] At 25% yearly incidence per firm and about DKK 40–70k per event, the expected loss is about DKK 10–17.5k per firm per year. A DKK 10–14k (ex VAT) lock would pay back in about 1 year if it prevents break-ins. How well these locks actually work is **not verified**.
- **Willingness to pay: evidence strength = Strong** for the problem and category spend (insurer payouts, insurer discount programmes, many installers with public prices). **Medium** for any new entrant: nothing shows unmet demand that the current players fail to serve.
- **Why switch / what a newcomer could add.** [Hyp] Possible angles: (a) a fleet-wide "theft-readiness" package (approved lock + tracker tags + insurance documentation + downtime kit or loan tools) sold per van per month; (b) a fitting service in Jutland regions with fewer installers; (c) a certified-parking / overnight secure-storage service. Why incumbents might not copy it: weak reason. The incumbents already fit on site, bundle with insurers and sell through wholesalers (Carl Ras). Any bundling idea is easy for them to copy.
- **Founder fit.** [Hyp] Direct sales and willingness to do physical work fit the job. There is no automotive, locksmith or trades background, and the founder has limited weekly time, while fitting jobs are 3–4 h and daytime-bound. Low fit with the education/board background.
- **Capital (estimates).** [Est] Test: DKK 5–15k (20 tradesperson interviews, a landing page, an agent/referral deal with an existing lock supplier). Launch as a mobile fitter: DKK 150–350k (used van, tools, lock stock, liability insurance, supplier training). Launch as a reseller/agent only: under DKK 25k.
- **Red flags.** Falling theft trend (−20% in 2025). Crowded, insurer-linked incumbents. Low repeat purchase. Vehicle-warranty and liability risk if a fitting goes wrong. Seasonal and regional demand (Copenhagen-heavy).
- **Preliminary ratings (1–5):** demand evidence 4; ability to reach buyers 3; founder fit 2; contribution margin 3; differentiation 2; execution feasibility 3; capital exposure 3; growth potential 2. **Confidence: medium.**

### Gaps

- F&P's formal lock-approval scheme (the "SSF 1051 class 5" reference is unverified; "Skafor" was not checked). Which insurers make approved locks a condition rather than a recommendation.

### Takeaway
Danish daycare institutions clearly buy cargo bikes through specialist institution suppliers, at about DKK 16.7k–38k per bike (list prices). But this session could not confirm the key questions: service spend, breakdown or downtime evidence, the Babboe recall's effect in Denmark, existing mobile fleet-service providers and prices, or municipal tenders. It could also be bundled across a municipality's daycare institutions and home-care e-bike fleets. Whether this already exists (e.g. suppliers' own service, local mobile bike mechanics) is **unknown**.
- **Founder fit.** [Hyp] Strong: 10+ years in specialist education, knowledge of how institution leaders and municipalities decide, direct sales, hands-on. Limited weekly time conflicts with a response-time promise unless the service is scheduled-only (planned inspection rounds).
- **Capital (estimates).** [Est] Test: DKK 10–25k (tools, mobile repair stand, parts float, a basic course; pilot with 3–5 institutions). Launch: DKK 75–200k (cargo-bike or small-van mobile workshop, parts stock, booking software, liability insurance).
- **Red flags.** [Hyp] Municipal framework agreements (SKI or local tenders) may lock service to incumbents. Institution supplier warranties may require the manufacturer's own service. Low price tolerance in public budgets. Density: the business works only in cities.

## Pair 3 — EV charging in andelsboligforeninger / ejerforeninger / almene boliger and small workplaces

### Takeaway
**No sourced evidence was collected in this session** (quota exhausted before this pair was reached; clever.dk could not be fetched). On the brief's own framing, there are several national operators (Clever, Spirii, Monta, E.ON Drive, Norlys, Eways). Installation also needs an authorised electrician (unverified background). So the working hypothesis is that this market is **too crowded and too capital-intensive for this founder**. The only low-capital angle is a neutral board-side advisory or tendering service. Confidence is low; this should be confirmed with a short, targeted check before being dropped.

### Cited Findings
- None collected: every search and fetch attempted for this pair failed (quota or proxy block). No prices, subsidies, right-to-charge rules or complaints can be cited from this session.

### Inferences
- **Problem / user / buyer.** [Hyp] Users: residents with EVs. Buyer and budget holder: the association board, with general-assembly approval (andel/ejerforening), or the housing organisation plus tenants' democracy (almene boliger). At workplaces, the owner or facility manager. Trigger: residents asking for charging, a new-build or renovation requirement, an operator's offer. Frequency: one-off installation plus a recurring subscription or operating contract.
- **Willingness to pay: evidence strength = not assessed in this session** (Medium is likely, because several operators compete with public prices, but **unverified**).
- **Newcomer angle.** [Hyp] A board advisor or tender facilitator that compares operator offers (ownership vs. subscription, lock-in terms, kWh settlement, meter setup) and runs the general-assembly process. This fits the founder's board experience and needs no electrical authorisation. Incumbents would not copy it because they sell their own contracts. Payment would likely be one-off, with low volume per customer.
- **Founder fit.** [Hyp] Board-process experience is relevant. There is no electrical authorisation, no energy background and limited time. The installer/operator model is a poor fit.
- **Capital (estimates).** [Est] Advisory test: under DKK 10k. Installer/operator launch: DKK 500k+, plus an authorised-electrician partnership and financing for the hardware. Highly uncertain.
- **Red flags.** [Hyp] Strong well-funded incumbents with 0-DKK-upfront subscription models. Regulatory and tax changes to electricity-tax refunds for charging operators (unverified). Long board decision cycles (general-assembly timing).
- **Preliminary ratings (1–5):** demand evidence 3; ability to reach buyers 2; founder fit 2; contribution margin 2; differentiation 1; execution feasibility 2; capital exposure 2 (installer model) or 5 (advisory model); growth potential 3. **Confidence: low.**

### Gaps
All of the following are unverified and need sources:
- Current installation and subscription prices for Clever, Spirii, Monta, E.ON Drive, Norlys and Eways for associations.
- Any Danish "right to charge" rules for owners, cooperative members or tenants. BR18 requirements for charging infrastructure in new or renovated buildings.
- Subsidy pools (Energistyrelsen, ens.dk) and changes to electricity-tax refunds for charging operators around 2026.
- Complaints (Trustpilot, Forbrugerrådet Tænk, association forums) about lock-in or pricing.
- Share of associations that already have charging (a saturation check).
- Authorisation requirement: whether installation needs an authorised electrician under the electrical authorisation law (elautorisationsloven, Sikkerhedsstyrelsen). **Background knowledge only; not confirmed here.**
