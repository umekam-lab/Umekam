## Pair 1 — Robust / anti-ligature ("indestructible") furniture and fixtures for bosteder, psychiatry, sikrede institutioner, opholdssteder, specialskoler, dagbehandling

### Takeaway
Danish anti-ligature furniture is a real, publicly purchased category **in regional psychiatry**. Arkisafe (Danish) holds announced agreements with Region Hovedstaden, Region Sjælland and Region Nordjylland, and Daarbak Design supplies regions through the D5R framework. I found **no public prices, no contract values and no damage/replacement-cost data**, and no evidence of organised spending in the municipal and private social sector (bosteder, opholdssteder, dagbehandling). That social-sector gap is both the possible opening and the main uncertainty. The Swedish specialist HealSafe sells directly only in Sweden and Norway and says it recruits distributors for other countries. No Danish distributor turned up.

### Inferences
**Problem, user and buyer [Hypothesis unless marked]**
- Problem: ordinary furniture in rooms and common areas gets broken, thrown or used as a weapon or ligature point by outward-reacting (udadreagerende) or self-harming residents or pupils. The result is injury risk, replacement cost, and "institutional", bare rooms that staff empty out to reduce risk.
- Users: residents or pupils, and staff (work-environment risk).
- Buyers and budget holders: (a) **regional psychiatry** through regional or D5R frameworks and project tenders ([Fact] that such agreements exist, per the findings above); (b) **municipal bosteder, sikrede institutioner and specialskoler**, where the institution leader buys from the operating budget within municipal or SKI agreements; (c) **private opholdssteder and private or selvejende bosteder**, where the owner or manager buys, probably outside public frameworks (to verify, see the procurement section).
- Purchase triggers: new builds and conversions, new residents with a high risk profile, an incident or AT/Socialtilsyn remark, and replacement after damage. Frequency is irregular and incident-driven, with new-build peaks.

**Alternatives, including doing nothing**
- Buying ordinary cheap furniture and replacing it often, stripping rooms bare, fixing furniture to walls or floors with local carpentry, custom joinery, or ordering psychiatry-grade products from Arkisafe, Daarbak or HealSafe. Doing nothing costs replacement spend, injuries, staff sick leave and turnover. **None of these costs could be quantified from sources.**

**Evidence strength: Medium for psychiatry** (observed regional agreements and tender activity, but no prices or values); **Weak for bosteder, opholdssteder and dagbehandling** (anecdote and work-environment context only).

**Why a customer might switch, and what a newcomer could offer [Hypothesis]**
- The offer: a **"normalised but robust" range sized for the social sector** (homely look, weighted or heavy pieces, rounded edges, fixable, repairable or replaceable covers) sold with pedagogical framing (low-arousal environments, KRAP/low-arousal language). Plus quick delivery and small orders, which suits a 6–12-place opholdssted that cannot run a tender.
- Why incumbents might not copy it: Arkisafe and Daarbak are built around regional psychiatry and framework tenders, where the sales motion (hundreds of small private buyers) differs. HealSafe has no Danish distributor, and a distributorship could be the fastest route in. Incumbents could still copy easily if the segment proves attractive, so differentiation would sit in channel and credibility, not product IP.

**Standards (general knowledge, NOT verified in this session, check before use)**: non-domestic seating and table strength and durability (EN 16139 / EN 1728 / EN 15372 / EN 1730), furniture fire ignitability (EN 1021-1/-2), and for fixtures, the building-product fire class (EN 13501-1). In the UK, anti-ligature hardware is often tested against DHF TS 001. No Danish anti-ligature standard or Sundhedsstyrelsen design guidance was found.

**Founder fit [Hypothesis]**: strong. The founder has daily experience of outward-reacting pupils in dagbehandling and specialskole, speaks the pedagogical and social-sector language, and has a board-level network in the private and selvejende sector. The documentation-tool background may help link purchases to incident documentation. The founder's weak point is furniture engineering and certification, so a distributor or white-label model fits better than own manufacturing.

**Capital (estimates)**: test 20–60k DKK (samples or demo pieces from a supplier such as HealSafe, visits and interviews at 15–25 opholdssteder and bosteder). Launch as distributor: 150–400k DKK (demo stock, product-liability insurance, catalogue and web). Own-design manufacturing: 0.5–2M DKK+ (design, strength and fire testing, minimum order quantities).

**Biggest red flags [Hypothesis]**: (1) the social sector may simply buy cheap IKEA-grade furniture and absorb the damage, with no budget line or willingness to pay a premium; (2) regional psychiatry, the proven segment, is locked into frameworks with Arkisafe and Daarbak; (3) product liability if a "safe" product is involved in a self-harm incident; (4) small, irregular orders.

**Preliminary ratings (1–5)**: demand evidence 3 · ability to reach buyers 3 · founder fit 4 · contribution margin 3 · differentiation 3 · execution feasibility 3 · capital exposure 3 · growth potential 3. **Confidence: low.**

### Gaps
- Arkisafe, Daarbak and HealSafe price lists, and contract values or durations for the Region Hovedstaden, Sjælland and Nordjylland agreements (pages could not be opened).
- Whether Kwalu, Knightsbridge or Pineapple have Danish or Nordic distributors.
- Any figure on damage or replacement costs in Danish bosteder, opholdssteder or psychiatry, or any Socialtilsynet finding on physical environments linked to violence.
- Sundhedsstyrelsen or regional design guidance for psychiatric units (anti-ligature requirements); number and capacity of sikrede institutioner.
- udbud.dk / TED tenders for "robuste møbler" from municipalities (not reached).

## Pair 2 — Low-stimulus learning spaces: acoustic pods, screened workstations, quiet booths for pupils with ADHD/autism

### Takeaway
There is a clear budget backdrop: municipalities budgeted about **DKK 14.4bn (2024)** for segregated special education, rising further in 2025, at about 27.8% of school spending. Many Danish suppliers already sell **screens and screened workstations (from about DKK 2,350 to about DKK 30,500)**, and office pods sell for **about DKK 28,000–66,000 excl. VAT** (commercial office-pod prices). I found **no evidence that Danish schools buy acoustic pods or booths for pupils**, and no school-specific pod product in Denmark. The school market today buys screens, not booths.

### Inferences
**Problem, user and buyer [Hypothesis]**
- Problem: pupils with ADHD, autism or sensory sensitivities in mainstream (inklusion) and special classes need somewhere to withdraw from noise and visual stimuli without leaving adult supervision. Classrooms and group rooms are too few, and "the corridor" is the de facto quiet space.
- User: the pupil. Staff are secondary users.
- Buyer: **skoleleder** from the school's decentralised operating budget (small items). Larger items go through the **municipal building or investment budget** at new builds or renovations (e.g., the Aarhus and Egedal specialskoler above). PPR (pedagogical-psychological advice) may recommend but usually does not pay.
- Triggers: new special class or specialskole, renovation, an individual pupil's inclusion plan, acoustics complaints. Frequency: project-driven, with a replacement cycle of 10 years or more.

**Alternatives**: do nothing (headphones, the corridor, group rooms), desk and floor screens at DKK 2–30k, soft "caves" or tents, bought or improvised; office pods at DKK 28–66k excl. VAT (not designed for children).

**Evidence strength: Medium** for screens (many competitors with public prices plus a large special-education budget). **Weak** for pupil pods or booths specifically (no observed purchases).

**Why switch, and what a newcomer could offer [Hypothesis]**: a child-specific booth whose design requirements come from specialist pedagogy: always visible to the adult (glazing and sightline), cannot be locked from inside, ventilated, calming light and colours, robust and cleanable, movable, and priced between screens and office pods (about DKK 12–25k). Office-pod makers are unlikely to design for children's safety needs. **Lekolar, AJ Produkter or Kinnarps could copy quickly**, so differentiation is weak unless there is a strong brand or evidence base.

**Standards (general knowledge, NOT verified, check before use)**: acoustic performance of pods (ISO 23351-1), fire classification of materials (EN 13501-1 / EN 1021), educational furniture (EN 1729), ventilation and indoor climate (BR18 building regulations), and the General Product Safety Regulation (EU 2023/988) if sold as a product for children. Also whether a pod with a door is a "room" under building rules.

**Founder fit [Hypothesis]**: good problem insight and credibility with school leaders and PPR; can run pilots in special settings. Industrial design and manufacturing are not the founder's strength, so this needs a production partner.

**Capital (estimates)**: test 30–100k DKK (prototype from a local joinery or acoustic firm, pilots in 2–3 schools). Launch: 300k–1M DKK (design iteration, fire and acoustic testing, first batch, demo units).

**Biggest red flags [Hypothesis]**: schools may prefer cheap screens and teacher-built "caves"; the product is bulky, freight- and installation-heavy; new builds go through architects and framework tenders; any restraint or seclusion perception (isolating a child) carries reputational and legal risk.

**Preliminary ratings (1–5)**: demand evidence 3 · reach 3 · founder fit 4 · contribution margin 3 · differentiation 2 · execution feasibility 2 · capital exposure 2 · growth potential 3. **Confidence: low.**

### Gaps
- Prices for Lekolar, Wesco, HABA Pro and Office Line quiet booths or "hideaways"; Framery, Silent Pod and Mikomax EUR price lists.
- Any Danish tender or news item showing schools buying pupil pods or booths.
- Special-education spend per pupil; number of special classes or centre classes (not reached).

## Pair 3 — Mobile-phone storage and locking for Danish schools

### Takeaway
Legal status as of 22 Sep 2026: the broad agreement of 30 Sep 2025 (mandatory local mobile-free policy from school year 2026/27) was introduced as **bill L 130 on 26 Feb 2026, but lapsed when the general election was called (held 24 Mar 2026)**, and was not re-introduced before summer. On about **8–10 Sep 2026**, after historically weak PISA results, the new government announced a **"total ban" on private screens in folkeskoler and frie grundskoler, covering lessons and breaks** (with exceptions such as diabetes monitoring). The bill is not yet in force. Many schools are already mobile-free voluntarily. The Danish product market is **crowded and low-ticket (about DKK 2,650–3,900 per 30-phone "mobilhotel")**, and a bag or box costs nothing. A policy wave is coming, but it is a commodity with a short window.

### Cited Findings
**Legal and policy timeline**
- [Fact] **30 Sep 2025**: an agreement between the then government (S, V, M) and SF, LA, K, DF and RV. School boards must adopt a **local policy making folkeskoler (0.–10. klasse) and SFO/fritidshjem (up to 3rd grade) mobile-free from school year 2026/27**, with the possibility of well-reasoned exceptions (health, pupils with disabilities). Computers and tablets used in teaching are exempt. A content-filtering requirement on school networks is also included — [UVM news 2025-09-30](https://uvm.dk/aktuelt/nyheder/2025/september/250930-ny-aftale-om-mobilfrie-folkeskoler-og-fritidstilbud/); [UVM agreement PDF (2025-09-30)](https://uvm.dk/media/3b4bd0mu/aftale-om-mobilfrie-folkeskoler-og-fritidstilbud-mv.pdf); [DR (2025-09-30)](https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/indland/folketingsflertal-er-enige-om-folkeskoler-skal-vaere-mobilfri)
- [Fact] The agreement builds on the **Trivselskommissionen** recommendations (February 2025): a legal requirement that all grundskoler (and SFO) be free of smartphones, smartwatches and private tablets, and that children not get a smartphone before age 13 — [Regeringen.dk (2025)](https://regeringen.dk/nyheder/2025/trivselskommissionens-anbefalinger-er-klar/); [DR (2025)](https://www.dr.dk/nyheder/indland/forbyd-mobiler-i-skolen-og-styrk-fysisk-samvaer-her-er-anbefalingerne-til-boerns)

- [Estimate/Inference] DKK 1,611,360 ÷ 7.46 ≈ **EUR 216,000**, which matches the EU sub-central supply threshold. The state-level goods threshold (lower) was not captured.
- [Hypothesis] Almost every purchase in Pairs 1–4 (a sensory room of DKK 50–500k, a few robust chairs, cabinets, screens) sits **below the EU threshold**. For municipal buyers, the binding constraint is **whether the municipality has committed to SKI's furniture agreement** (and which sub-agreement covers "care" or "school" furniture). A newcomer's product then either (a) gets carried by one of the three SKI suppliers per sub-agreement (Lekolar Leika for school furniture), (b) is positioned as "special equipment not covered by the framework", or (c) targets buyers outside the frameworks.
- [Hypothesis, needs legal check] **Private opholdssteder and private bosteder** (financed by municipal per-place payments) are generally not contracting authorities and can buy freely. **Selvejende institutions with operating agreements** (driftsoverenskomst) may count as bodies governed by public law and fall under the framework commitments of the municipality they contract with. This makes private and selvejende tilbud the **most reachable segment** for a direct-selling founder in Pairs 1 and 4.
